# EudaiLabs Project Ethics Framework

This framework evaluates a project through four questions. Each one names the concern, defines the threshold, and states how the project should be categorized when that threshold is met.

## P1: Physical Harm

**Core question:** Is the system intended to cause physical harm to human beings who do not represent a direct threat?

**Purpose** means the system's primary or explicit function. It does not include side effects or possible misuse.

**Physical harm** means bodily injury, illness, death, or verifiable physical deterioration.

**Human beings** means any organism with its own complete human genome, from fertilization until natural death.

**Direct threat** means a human being who is currently causing, is about to cause, or will with certainty cause physical harm to another person.

**Exception: harm reduction in abortion.** A system whose purpose is to make an abortion procedure safer, such as through clinical information, medical follow-up, or access to healthcare professionals, does not trigger this question. Its function is to reduce physical harm, not to cause it. This exception does not apply to systems that promote, encourage, or facilitate the decision to have an abortion.

**What this means:** a project that meets this condition belongs in Category I: Unacceptable.

## P2: Exploitative Behavioral Dependence

**Core question:** Does the effectiveness or profitability of the system depend on generating harmful behaviors in the user through the exploitation of cognitive biases?

**Harmful behaviors** include compulsive use, or use that is difficult to stop through one's own will. They also include revenue generation through repeated spending induced by uncertainty mechanisms, variable rewards, or expectations of gain, as well as foreseeable deterioration of attention, self-control, or the ability to make deliberate decisions.

**Exploitation of cognitive biases** means the intentional use of known psychological mechanisms to induce the behaviors described above.

This can include variable or unpredictable reward loops, infinite scrolling or continuous consumption without natural stopping points, asymmetric friction where it is easy to enter and difficult to leave, artificial urgency, FOMO, manipulative time pressure, or personalization primarily aimed at maximizing usage, time spent, or spending.

**What this means:** a project that meets this condition belongs in Category I: Unacceptable.

## P3: High-Impact Decisions

**Core question:** Does the system make, or decisively influence, decisions with significant consequences for people's lives?

**Significant consequences** are decisions that meaningfully affect a person's opportunities, living conditions, safety, health, education, employment, property, or freedom.

**Decisively influence** means the final decision depends primarily on the outcome produced by the system, even when limited human involvement exists.

**What this means:** a project that meets this condition belongs in Category II: Debatable.

## P4: Net Improvement Under Typical Use

**Core question:** Considering typical use, does society or the user end up in a better position than where they started?

**Typical use** means the expected behavior of the majority of users, not the ideal use case or an extreme one.

**Better position** means that at least one concrete indicator improves as a result of using the system: what improves, for whom, and how that improvement can be observed. This may include capability, well-being, efficiency, safety, or harm reduction.

**Harm reduction criterion:** a system that does not eliminate a problem but reduces its negative impact still counts as an improvement. For example, a platform providing information about safe abortion does not promote abortion, but it reduces harm for those who have already made that decision.

**What this means:** when typical use leaves society or the user in a better position, the project belongs in Category III-A: Priority. When there is no improvement, but no harm either, it belongs in Category III-B: Acceptable, not a priority.
