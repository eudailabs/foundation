# EudaiLabs Project Ethics Framework


## P1 — Is the system intended to cause physical harm to human beings who do not represent a direct threat?

**Purpose:** the primary or explicit function of the system, not a side effect or a possible misuse.

**Physical harm:** bodily injury, illness, death, or verifiable physical deterioration.

**Human beings:** any organism with its own complete human genome, from fertilization until natural death.

**Direct threat:** a human being who is currently causing, is about to cause, or will with certainty cause physical harm to another person.

**Exception — harm reduction in abortion:** a system whose purpose is to make an abortion procedure safer (clinical information, medical follow-up, access to healthcare professionals) does not trigger this question, because its function is to reduce physical harm, not to cause it. This exception does not apply to systems that promote, encourage, or facilitate the decision to have an abortion.

**Result:**

- Yes → Cat. I (Unacceptable)

---

## P2 — Does the effectiveness or profitability of the system depend on generating harmful behaviors in the user through the exploitation of cognitive biases?

**Harmful behaviors include:**

- Compulsive use or use that is difficult to stop through one's own will
- Revenue generation through repeated spending induced by uncertainty mechanisms, variable rewards, or expectations of gain
- Foreseeable deterioration of attention, self-control, or the ability to make deliberate decisions

**Exploitation of cognitive biases:** the intentional use of known psychological mechanisms to induce the behaviors described above, including:

- Variable or unpredictable reward loops
- Infinite scrolling or continuous consumption without natural stopping points
- Asymmetric friction (easy to enter, difficult to leave)
- Artificial urgency, FOMO, or manipulative time pressure
- Personalization primarily aimed at maximizing usage, time spent, or spending

**Result:**

- Yes → Cat. I (Unacceptable)

---

## P3 — Does the system make, or decisively influence, decisions with significant consequences for people's lives?

**Significant consequences:** decisions that meaningfully affect a person's opportunities, living conditions, safety, health, education, employment, property, or freedom.

**Decisively influence:** the final decision depends primarily on the outcome produced by the system, even if limited human involvement exists.

**Result:**

- Yes → Cat. II (Debatable)

---

## P4 — Considering typical use, does society or the user end up in a better position than where they started?

**Typical use:** the expected behavior of the majority of users, not the ideal use case nor an extreme one.

**Better position:** at least one concrete indicator improves as a result of using the system: what improves, for whom, and how it is observable. This may include capability, well-being, efficiency, safety, or harm reduction.

**Harm reduction criterion:** a system that does not eliminate a problem but reduces its negative impact still counts as an improvement. For example, a platform providing information about safe abortion does not promote abortion, but it reduces harm for those who have already made that decision.

**Result:**

- Yes → Cat. III-A (Priority)

- No improvement, but no harm either → Cat. III-B (Acceptable, not a priority)
